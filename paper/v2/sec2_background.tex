\subsection{Digital Twins of Vehicles, Fleets, and Networks}\label{subsec:bg_dt}
Kritzinger et al.\ classify virtual representations by the automation of the data flows between a physical object and its representation. A digital model exchanges data with the object only through manual flows, a digital shadow (DS) receives the data of the object automatically, and a DT exchanges data with the object automatically in both directions [kritzinger2018digital]. Grieves and Vickers define the DTP, which describes a prototypical physical artifact; the DTI, which describes one physical product and remains linked to this product throughout its life; the DTA, the aggregation of the DTIs; and the DTE, the application space in which applications operate on the DTs [grieves2017]. The National Academies require the verification, validation, and uncertainty quantification of a DT for the purpose of the DT [nasem2024foundational], and Kapteyn et al.\ update the digital state of an aircraft in flight by Bayesian inference over a probabilistic graphical model [kapteyn2021probabilistic].

Peng et al.\ [Peng2023] and Wang et al.\ [wang2026Marine] surveyed DTs for unmanned surface and underwater vehicles, and most single-vehicle works implement a DS. Bhagavathi et al.\ [Bhagavathi2023] fed sensor data of an autonomous surface vehicle into fault diagnosis models, Berg et al.\ [berg2025Syncing] synchronized the twin of an autonomous surface vehicle by parameter identification, and Xu et al.\ [xu2025Dynamic] sampled the state of a ship on events to reduce the ship-to-shore traffic. For fleets, Yan et al.\ [yan2026Swarm] and Zhang et al.\ [zhang2024Formation] assigned one twin to each underwater vehicle of a swarm or formation, Kim et al.\ [kim2026Command] validated the command-and-control system of five USVs in a DT, Wang et al.\ [wang2026Optimizing] trained a cruising policy in a DT of the fleet, and Couceiro et al.\ [couceiro2026Paradigm] ran a twin of an aquatic robot swarm over a Wi-Fi ad hoc network; Baniqued et al.\ [Baniqued2024], Mtowe et al.\ [mtowe2025Edge], and Hartwell et al.\ [Hartwell2024] twinned robot and aero-engine fleets, and Gervasi et al.\ [gervasi2026Systematic] classified systems of DTs. These twins serve as validation or training environments or as control models, leave the transport unspecified, and state no deadline for the update of the twins.

Zhou et al.\ describe the network digital twin (NDT) with four elements: the data of the state of the network, the models that emulate changes of the network, the mapping that links the NDT to the network in both directions, and the interfaces to the network and to the applications [zhou2026ndt]. Wu et al.\ survey digital twin networks [wu2021digital]. Almasan et al.\ use graph neural networks (GNNs) as the models of an NDT for what-if analysis [almasan2022network], RouteNet predicts the delay, the jitter, and the loss of each path of wired topologies [rusek2020routenet], and Almeida et al.\ built a DT of a Wi-Fi network in ns-3 with a learned propagation loss, without a link to the network in operation [almeida2023position]. Fleet-DT twins one Wi-Fi network in operation, in which each vessel reaches the GS in one hop, and predicts the capacity of its channel in each step from the measurements of this network.

\subsection{MQTT and the Transport of the Data of Digital Twins}\label{subsec:bg_mqtt}
In MQTT, a client publishes messages on topics to a broker over TCP, and the broker delivers each message to the clients that subscribe to its topic [oasis2014mqtt], and an MQTT bridge connects two brokers over one client connection and forwards the messages of selected topics [eclipse2026mosquittoconf]. With QoS~0, the sender sends the PUBLISH packet once. With QoS~1, the receiver acknowledges the PUBLISH packet with a PUBACK packet. With QoS~2, the sender and the receiver exchange the PUBLISH, PUBREC, PUBREL, and PUBCOMP packets. After a reconnection with a persistent session (CleanSession set to 0), each side resends the unacknowledged PUBLISH packets of QoS~1 and~2 [oasis2014mqtt].

Zhang et al.\ [Zhang2024] compared DDS, MQTT, and Zenoh under ROS~2 over wired and wireless links, Freitas et al.\ [freitas2026Distribution] evaluated five middlewares for the synchronization of DTs on a degraded network, Lima et al.\ [lima2025Bridge] measured the effect of MQTT bridge architectures on latency and reliability, and Li et al.\ [li2023Remote] carried the sensor data of a research vessel over MQTT to a control center. An et al.\ [an2025enabling] updated the DTs of a simulated UAV network over MQTT with a lower age of information than other transports. Herrero [herrero:2023] transported media packets in MQTT messages, RTP [rfc3550] carries media in its own session, and Kremer et al.\ [kremer2023Teleoperated] and Mtowe et al.\ [mtowe2025Edge] replaced the video with a twin of the environment or with edge features. Shahri et al.\ [shahri2022Extending] reserved bandwidth for each MQTT flow through SDN, Palmese et al.\ [palmese2022Adaptive] adjusted the QoS level of each MQTT-SN node, the DDS DEADLINE policy [omg2015Dds] signals missed periodic updates, and Jaiswal et al.\ [jaiswal2023wiros] scheduled the ROS~2 topics of one robot and configured its IEEE~802.11 contention parameters. Fleet-DT carries the data of each vessel over the data bridge of the vessel and admits, in each step, the data of every vessel within the capacity that the DT of the network predicts.

\subsection{Contention for a Shared Wireless Channel}\label{subsec:cong}
In IEEE~802.11, the access point (AP) and its stations contend for one channel with the distributed coordination function (DCF) [bianchi2000performance]. Bianchi models the backoff of each of $n$ saturated stations with a Markov chain and obtains the probability $\tau$ that a station transmits in a slot and the probability $p_{\mathrm{c}}$ that a transmitted frame collides from
\begin{equation}
\tau = \frac{2\,(1 - 2p_{\mathrm{c}})}{(1 - 2p_{\mathrm{c}})(\mathrm{CW}_{\min} + 2) + p_{\mathrm{c}} (\mathrm{CW}_{\min} + 1) \big(1 - (2p_{\mathrm{c}})^{m_{\mathrm{b}}}\big)}, \quad p_{\mathrm{c}} = 1 - (1 - \tau)^{n-1},
\label{eq:bianchi}
\end{equation}
where $m_{\mathrm{b}}$ is the maximum backoff stage [bianchi2000performance]. With $P_{\mathrm{tr}} = 1 - (1 - \tau)^n$, the probability that a slot holds a transmission, and $P_{\mathrm{s}} = n\tau(1-\tau)^{n-1}/P_{\mathrm{tr}}$, the probability that this transmission succeeds, the fraction of time in which the channel carries successful transmissions is
\begin{equation}
\eta(n) = \frac{P_{\mathrm{tr}} P_{\mathrm{s}}\, T_{\mathrm{succ}}}{(1 - P_{\mathrm{tr}})\,T_{\mathrm{slot}} + P_{\mathrm{tr}} P_{\mathrm{s}}\, T_{\mathrm{succ}} + P_{\mathrm{tr}}(1 - P_{\mathrm{s}})\, T_{\mathrm{coll}}},
\label{eq:eff}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{slot}}$ is the slot time, $T_{\mathrm{succ}}$ is the duration of a successful transmission (PHY header, MAC header and payload, SIFS, acknowledgment, and DIFS), and $T_{\mathrm{coll}}$ is the duration of a collision (PHY header, MAC header and payload, and DIFS); the saturation throughput of Bianchi is~\eqref{eq:eff} with the transmission time of the mean payload in the numerator [bianchi2000performance]. The ns-3 Model Library compares the throughput of the ns-3 Wi-Fi model with~\eqref{eq:eff} for IEEE~802.11a, b, and g [nsnam2020wifi]. Heusse et al.\ show that a station at a lower bit rate reduces the throughput of every station of the cell [heusse2003performance], and Tobagi and Kleinrock show that hidden terminals, stations that do not sense each other, degrade the throughput of carrier sense multiple access [tobagi1975packet]. TCP delivers one ordered byte stream per connection [rfc9293], so a segment that waits in a queue delays every later byte of its connection.

The enhanced distributed channel access (EDCA) of IEEE~802.11 keeps one queue per access category, each with its own arbitration interframe space (AIFS) and contention window, which take the places of DIFS and $\mathrm{CW}_{\min}$ in~\eqref{eq:bianchi} and~\eqref{eq:eff}; IEEE~802.11 maps the user priority (UP) of a frame to the access category, with UP~6 and~7 in AC\_VO and UP~0 and~3 in AC\_BE, and RFC~8325 maps the differentiated services code point (DSCP) of an IP packet to the UP [ieee80211std2020, rfc8325]. With the optional HCCA, the AP admits or rejects each flow that a station declares in a TSPEC and polls the stations of the admitted flows [ieee80211std2020]. Leith et al.\ show that, under the DCF, the AP receives about the share of the transmission opportunities of one station, although the AP sends the TCP acknowledgments of every upload, and that EDCA parameters that give the acknowledgments at the AP a shorter AIFS and a smaller $\mathrm{CW}_{\min}$ restore the fairness between uploads and downloads [leith2005tcp]. H{\o}iland-J{\o}rgensen et al.\ schedule the queues of the stations at the AP by airtime in the Linux kernel [hoiland2017ending].

The available bandwidth of a link is the capacity that the current traffic leaves unused [prasad2003bandwidth]. The ETSI DCC adapts the transmissions of each vehicle to the channel busy ratio (CBR) over 100~ms [etsi2018dcc]; Sarr et al.\ [sarr2008bandwidth] and Yang and Kravets [yang2005contention] admit a flow of an ad hoc network when the available bandwidth that the idle time of the channel indicates covers the rate of the flow; and Winstein et al.\ send over a cellular link the bytes that a low quantile of a forecast of the link allows [winstein2013stochastic]. These works estimate the capacity per link, node, or station and serve flows with a rate. In the time-triggered architecture, the nodes act at instants of a global time base that a clock synchronization establishes [kopetz2003time], and a firm deadline makes a result useless after the deadline [kopetz2011real].

\subsection{Allocation of a Shared Medium}\label{subsec:dba}
In dynamic bandwidth allocation (DBA), a controller divides the capacity of a shared medium among the stations in each cycle from the demand that each station reports. The optical line terminal of an EPON grants each unit a window per polling cycle [kramer2002ipact], and the base station of IEEE~802.16 grants the uplink bandwidth of each frame [ieee80216std2017]. Above the IEEE~802.11 MAC, Rao and Stoica allocate equal time slots among the nodes with loosely synchronized clocks [rao2005overlay], Djukic and Mohapatra run TDMA schedules over commodity hardware with clocks synchronized within microseconds [djukic2009soft], and RT-WiFi builds a TDMA MAC on the timing synchronization function [wei2013rtwifi]. IEEE~802.11ax schedules uplink transmissions on resource units with trigger frames and target wake time [ieee80211ax2021, khorov2019tutorial], and IEEE~802.11be adds restricted target wake time for latency-sensitive traffic [ieee80211be2024, adame2021tsn]. These schemes give slots or resource units to stations, and each station selects the data of its slot. Network calculus bounds a flow with an arrival curve, which gives, for each duration, the largest number of bytes that the flow sends in an interval of this duration [cruz1991calculus, leboudec2001network]. Fleet-DT admits the data of every vessel within one airtime budget per step in the order of need of the GS, on radios with EDCA, and compares this admission with an overlay TDMA (Section~\ref{sec:method}).

\subsection{One-Step Prediction, Adaptive Conformal Inference, and Age of Information}\label{subsec:aci}
Exponential smoothing with the gain $\lambda \in (0, 1]$ predicts the next value of a measured series $y(t)$ with $\hat{y}(t+1) = (1 - \lambda)\,\hat{y}(t) + \lambda\, y(t)$, which minimizes the mean squared error of the one-step prediction of a random walk observed with noise for a gain that depends on the ratio of the two variances [harvey1989forecasting]; TCP predicts the round-trip time with the gain $1/8$ [rfc6298].

Conformal prediction turns a point predictor into a prediction set $\hat{C}_t(\alpha) = \{y : S_t(y) \leq \hat{Q}_t(1 - \alpha)\}$, where $S_t$ is a nonconformity score and $\hat{Q}_t$ the empirical quantile of the past scores, and the set covers the next observation with probability at least $1 - \alpha$ when the observations are exchangeable [gibbs2021adaptive]. Adaptive conformal inference (ACI) requires no exchangeability: with $\mathrm{err}_t = 1$ when the observation of step $t$ falls outside $\hat{C}_t(\alpha_t)$ and $\mathrm{err}_t = 0$ otherwise, ACI updates the level with
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{t+1} = \alpha_t + \gamma\,(\beta - \mathrm{err}_t),
\label{eq:aci_bg}
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ is the target miscoverage and $\gamma > 0$ is the step, and the conventions $\hat{Q}_t(x) = \infty$ for $x > 1$ and $\hat{Q}_t(x) = -\infty$ for $x < 0$ keep $\alpha_t \in [-\gamma, 1 + \gamma]$, so $\big|\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathrm{err}_t - \beta\big| \leq (\max\{\alpha_1, 1 - \alpha_1\} + \gamma)/(\gamma T)$ for every sequence of observations [gibbs2021adaptive].

The age of information (AoI) of a status at a monitor is the time elapsed since the release of the newest status that the monitor has received [kaul2012real]. Yates et al.\ survey the AoI of queues and of sources that share a channel [yates2021age], and Li et al.\ place the twins of mobile objects on edge servers to minimize the AoI of the queries [li2024aoi]. These works minimize an average AoI, and Fleet-DT bounds the AoI of the state of every vessel in each step with a sufficient capacity (Section~\ref{subsec:admission}).
